Geography & Infrastructure
Landlocked, borders DE, PL, SK, AT. Deeply integrated into German supply chains. EU and NATO member since 2004, Schengen since 2007. The CZK is freely convertible but CNB maintains a managed-float regime with occasional verbal/FX intervention.
Political Economy
ANO (centrist-populist) under Andrej Babiš leads opinion polls heading into the 2025 parliamentary election. The current SPOLU coalition has delivered fiscal consolidation (deficit ~2.2% GDP) but faces voter fatigue. Euro adoption remains a distant prospect — public opinion is 65%+ opposed. Key risk: ANO's policy platform is light on fiscal detail.
Society & Demographics
Population ~10.9mn, ageing profile. Highest GDP per capita in CEE (PPP-adjusted above Italy/Spain). Very low unemployment (~2.6%, EU's lowest) creates severe labour shortages in manufacturing and services. Prague property market is among Europe's most overvalued on price-to-income. Secular/atheist-leaning population; high social trust. Strong vocational education system supports manufacturing base.

MPC: CNB Bank Board — 7 members (Governor + 2 Vice Governors + 4 Board members). All appointed by the President. Decisions by simple majority.

Meetings: Eight monetary-policy meetings per year (roughly every 6 weeks). Meeting dates published 12 months in advance. Press conference and statement (in Czech and English) after each meeting. Quarterly Inflation Report (February, May, August, November).

Policy Framework
Inflation-targeting regime since 1998. Target: 2.0% CPI ±1pp tolerance band. CNB is the most transparent CEE central bank — it publishes the interest-rate forecast path as a fan chart, a practice matched only by the Riksbank and Norges Bank among European CBs. The CNB used an FX floor (EURCZK ≥27) as an additional easing tool from 2013-2017, accumulating ~€70bn in reserves during that period. Since 2017 the CZK has floated freely, with only occasional verbal intervention. CNB balance sheet is ~60% of GDP, inflated by the legacy FX-intervention reserves.
Balance Sheet

Total Assets: ~CZK 4.5 trillion (~60% GDP)

  • FX reserves: ~€140bn (65% of assets) — the largest reserve stock relative to GDP in the EU, legacy of the 2013-17 FX floor
  • Gold reserves: ~50 tonnes (rapidly built up under Governor Michl, from ~11t in 2022)
  • Government securities: minimal (~CZK 50bn) — CNB does not conduct QE
  • Liabilities: bank reserves (~CZK 3.8tn) are the dominant funding source, earning the discount rate
  • Capital position: strong positive equity; the large FX reserve stock generates valuation gains/losses that flow through the P&L
Key Risks & Vulnerabilities
(1) FX reserve valuation: the €140bn reserve stock is a macro insurance policy but also a massive carry-trade exposure — CNB is long EUR assets funded by short CZK liabilities at a negative carry of ~200bp. Each 1% CZK appreciation generates ~CZK 45bn in valuation losses. (2) Governor Michl's unconventional views: he is a well-known gold bug who has tripled CNB's gold holdings; while this diversifies reserves, some view it as outside the CB mainstream. (3) Ultra- tight labour market: unemployment at 2.6% is a structural inflation driver — wage growth above 7% despite CPI below 3% means the CNB must maintain a restrictive stance even as growth disappoints. (4) Policy divergence within CEE: CNB cut rates aggressively in 2024 (from 7.0% to 4.0%), ahead of NBP and MNB; if inflation re-accelerates, the CNB may be forced into a humiliating reversal.
